1. US Traffic Turnaround: Transactions vs. Ticket, and What It Costs
Starbucks' "Back to Starbucks" plan bet on rebuilding North American transaction (traffic) growth after several quarters of decline; the FY2025 10-K shows the hole the company was digging out of, while FY2026 press releases show comparable-transaction growth returning, but the analyst must weigh how much of the swing is pricing/mix versus genuine traffic recovery and what margin cost it exacted.
[a] Establish the pre-turnaround trough: FY2025 North America comparable store sales declined 2%, driven by a 4% decline in comparable transactions, partially offset by 2% ticket growth from pricing annualization, and connect it to the Green Apron Service model and staffing investments described as the "Back to Starbucks" response.
[b] Track the inflection using the two most recent quarters: Q1 FY26 delivered U.S. comparable transaction growth "for the first time in eight quarters" (transactions +3%, comps +4%), and Q2 FY26 accelerated further (comps +7.1%, transactions +4.3%, ticket +2.7%, with the ticket gain partly attributed to higher delivery sales).
[c] Weigh the margin cost of the turnaround: North America operating margin contracted 830 basis points to 11.5% in FY2025, driven by deleverage, restructuring/store-closure costs, and "Back to Starbucks" labor investment (~180bps) — assess whether the traffic recovery is paying for itself yet or is still margin-dilutive.

2. China Comparable Sales Deceleration and Competitive Discounting
China swung from strong transaction-led growth in Q1 FY26 to a near-stall in Q2 FY26, with average ticket turning negative — a pattern consistent with the "increased promotional activity" that already compressed International margins in FY2025 — raising the question of whether Starbucks is defending share by discounting into local competition rather than winning on demand.
[a] Quantify the deceleration: China comparable store sales rose 7% in Q1 FY26 (transactions +5%, ticket +2%), then slowed to just 0.5% in Q2 FY26, with transactions +2.1% but average ticket declining 1.6%.
[b] Tie the ticket decline to the competitive/pricing environment named in the 10-K: FY2025 International operating margin contracted 210bps to 12.1%, with roughly 170bps attributed to "increased promotional activity," and the Risk Factors section flags competitor "pricing actions (including price reductions, promotions, discounting...)" as a named risk to the business.
[c] Reconcile the two data points into a single read: transactions holding up while ticket falls suggests Starbucks may be buying volume/share stabilization in China through discounting rather than pure demand strength — take a position on whether this is a sustainable defense or a margin problem being deferred.

Rather than fight the competitive/traffic pressure in China alone, Starbucks sold down 60% of its China retail operations to Boyu Capital; the analyst must judge whether this is a vote of confidence in a local partner's ability to compete, or a capital-light retreat from a market Starbucks could not win outright on its own economics.
